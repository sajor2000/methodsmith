# PRISMA-S Checklist for Reporting Literature Searches

## Contents

- Information Sources and Methods (items 1-7)
- Search Strategies (items 8-13)
- Peer Review (item 14)
- Managing Records (items 15-16)

Reporting guideline extension for reporting literature searches in systematic reviews (PRISMA-S).
16-item checklist covering information sources and methods, search strategies, peer review of searches, and record management. PRISMA-S extends PRISMA 2020: apply it with the PRISMA core (or the subtype extension core), never as a stand-alone review checklist. It also applies to documenting planned searches in review protocols together with PRISMA-P.

**Primary reference:** Rethlefsen ML, Kirtley S, Waffenschmidt S, et al. PRISMA-S: an extension to the PRISMA statement for reporting literature searches in systematic reviews. J Med Libr Assoc. 2021;109(2):174-200. doi:10.5195/jmla.2021.962. PMID: 34285662.

**Source and licence:** All 16 items, section groupings, and item descriptions were verified on 2026-10-19 against Table 1 of the PRISMA-S paper, available in full at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8270366/. The article is licensed CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/; confirmed on the PMC page and in Crossref), so item descriptions below are quoted verbatim with attribution. The "What to look for" bullets are methodsmith review guidance, not PRISMA-S content.

Each item includes the item number, official name, description, and what to look for in analysis code and outputs when evaluating compliance.

## Information Sources and Methods (items 1-7)

### Item 1 -- Database name

**Description:** Name each individual database searched, stating the platform for each.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Search script or log naming each database and its platform (for example, Embase on Elsevier)
- Registry or config listing every database searched

### Item 2 -- Multi-database searching

**Description:** If databases were searched simultaneously on a single platform, state the name of the platform, listing all of the databases searched.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Platform name with the full database list when one platform searched several databases
- Multi-database search configuration retained

### Item 3 -- Study registries

**Description:** List any study registries searched.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Registry searches (for example, ClinicalTrials.gov) listed in the search log
- Registry names in the methods or supplementary search documentation

### Item 4 -- Online resources and browsing

**Description:** Describe any online or print source purposefully searched or browsed (e.g., tables of contents, print conference proceedings, websites), and how this was done.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Manually browsed sources named with the browsing method and dates
- Website or table-of-contents scans documented in search records

### Item 5 -- Citation searching

**Description:** Indicate whether cited references or citing references were examined, and describe any methods used for locating cited/citing references (e.g., browsing reference lists, using a citation index, setting up email alerts for references citing included studies).

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Forward and backward citation-search method described (index used, dates, alerts)
- Citation-search counts separated from database results

### Item 6 -- Contacts

**Description:** Indicate whether additional studies or data were sought by contacting authors, experts, manufacturers, or others.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Contact attempts (authors, experts, manufacturers) documented
- Responses and any studies obtained recorded

### Item 7 -- Other methods

**Description:** Describe any additional information sources or search methods used.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Any further search method (hand-search, listserv, unpublished data requests) described

## Search Strategies (items 8-13)

### Item 8 -- Full search strategies

**Description:** Include the search strategies for each database and information source, copied and pasted exactly as run.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Exact query strings for every database included as supplementary material or in the report
- Search scripts preserved with the project outputs

### Item 9 -- Limits and restrictions

**Description:** Specify that no limits were used, or describe any limits or restrictions applied to a search (e.g., date or time period, language, study design) and provide justification for their use.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Limits (dates, language, design filters) stated with justification
- Explicit "no limits" statement when none applied

### Item 10 -- Search filters

**Description:** Indicate whether published search filters were used (as originally designed or modified), and if so, cite the filter(s) used.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Published filters (for example, a Cochrane RCT filter) cited by source
- Modifications to a filter described

### Item 11 -- Prior work

**Description:** Indicate when search strategies from other literature reviews were adapted or reused for a substantive part or all of the search, citing the previous review(s).

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Reused strategies attributed to the earlier review
- Adaptations from the prior strategy noted

### Item 12 -- Updates

**Description:** Report the methods used to update the search(es) (e.g., rerunning searches, email alerts).

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Update method and date window documented for each updated search
- Alert-based monitoring described where used

### Item 13 -- Dates of searches

**Description:** For each search strategy, provide the date when the last search occurred.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Last-run date recorded per database and strategy
- Search timestamps in logs or manuscript methods

## Peer Review (item 14)

### Item 14 -- Peer review

**Description:** Describe any search peer review process.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Peer review of the strategy documented (for example, a PRESS-based review) with reviewer role

## Managing Records (items 15-16)

### Item 15 -- Total records

**Description:** Document the total number of records identified from each database and other information sources.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Per-source record counts in a flow table or diagram
- Counts consistent with the deduplicated and screened totals

### Item 16 -- Deduplication

**Description:** Describe the processes and any software used to deduplicate records from multiple database searches and other information sources.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Deduplication method and software named (for example, a reference manager or a script)
- Duplicate counts reported
