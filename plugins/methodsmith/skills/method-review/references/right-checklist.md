Reporting Items for practice Guidelines in HealThcare (RIGHT).
22-item checklist for reporting practice guidelines in health care, covering basic information (items 1-4), background (items 5-9), evidence (items 10-12), recommendations (items 13-15), review and quality assurance (items 16-17), funding and declaration and management of interests (items 18-19), and other information (items 20-22).

**Primary reference:** Chen Y, Yang K, Marušić A, et al. A Reporting Tool for Practice Guidelines in Health Care: The RIGHT Statement. Ann Intern Med. 2017;166(2):128-132. doi:10.7326/M16-1565. PMID: 27893062.

**Source and licence:** Item numbers, sub-item IDs, section domains, and item wording were verified on 2026-10-19 against the RIGHT checklist table in the Annals of Internal Medicine paper (https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M16-1565). The Annals article is copyright the American College of Physicians and carries no open licence (checked 2026-10-19 via Crossref; no Creative Commons licence is recorded for it), so the item descriptions below are faithful paraphrases that keep every element of the official item. Use the Annals paper or the EQUATOR-hosted RIGHT materials for verbatim wording. The "What to look for" bullets are methodsmith review guidance, not RIGHT content.

Each item includes the item number, topic, description, and what to look for in the guideline document and its development records when evaluating compliance.

## Basic information (items 1-4)

### Item 1a -- Title/subtitle

**Description:** Identify the report as a guideline by using the word "guideline(s)" or "recommendation(s)" in the title.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Guideline title or report header containing "guideline" or "recommendation"

### Item 1b -- Title/subtitle

**Description:** State the year the guideline was published.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Publication year in the title or document metadata

### Item 1c -- Title/subtitle

**Description:** State what the guideline focuses on, such as screening, diagnosis, treatment, management, prevention, or another area.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Focus stated in the title, subtitle, or summary header

### Item 2 -- Executive summary

**Description:** Summarize the recommendations the guideline contains.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Executive summary section listing the key recommendations

### Item 3 -- Abbreviations and acronyms

**Description:** Define new or key terms and list the abbreviations and acronyms used, where applicable.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Abbreviation list or glossary in the guideline document

### Item 4 -- Corresponding developer

**Description:** Name at least one corresponding developer or author who can answer questions about the guideline.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Named corresponding contact with affiliation or email

## Background (items 5-9)

### Item 5 -- Brief description of the health problem(s)

**Description:** Outline the basic epidemiology of the health problem: prevalence or incidence, morbidity, mortality, and the resulting burden, financial burden included.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Background section quantifying the problem's epidemiology and burden

### Item 6 -- Aim(s) of the guideline and specific objectives

**Description:** State the guideline's aim(s) and specific objectives, such as better health indicators (for example, mortality or disease prevalence), quality of life, or cost savings.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Objectives section naming measurable aims

### Item 7a -- Target population(s)

**Description:** Describe the main population(s) affected by the guideline's recommendation(s).

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Target population defined with eligibility boundaries (age, condition, setting)

### Item 7b -- Target population(s)

**Description:** Describe any subgroups the guideline gives special consideration.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Subgroup definitions where recommendations differ

### Item 8a -- End users and settings

**Description:** Describe who the guideline is mainly written for (such as primary care providers, clinical specialists, public health practitioners, program managers, and policymakers) and who else might use it.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Intended users listed in the guideline introduction

### Item 8b -- End users and settings

**Description:** State the setting(s) the guideline is meant for -- for instance primary care, inpatient facilities, or low- and middle-income countries.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Applicability settings stated

### Item 9a -- Guideline development groups

**Description:** Explain how every contributor to guideline development was chosen and give their roles and responsibilities (for example steering group, guideline panel, external reviewers, systematic review team, and methodologists).

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Development-group composition and selection process documented

### Item 9b -- Guideline development groups

**Description:** List everyone who took part in developing the guideline, with their title, role(s), and institutional affiliation(s).

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Full contributor list with affiliations and roles

## Evidence (items 10-12)

### Item 10a -- Health care questions

**Description:** State the key questions underpinning the recommendations, in PICO (population, intervention, comparator, outcome) or another suitable format.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Key questions listed in PICO or equivalent format

### Item 10b -- Health care questions

**Description:** Explain how the outcomes were chosen and ordered.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Outcome selection and prioritization method stated

### Item 11a -- Systematic reviews

**Description:** State whether the guideline rests on new systematic reviews done specifically for it or on existing systematic reviews.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Evidence-base origin (new or existing reviews) stated

### Item 11b -- Systematic reviews

**Description:** When existing systematic reviews were used, cite them and explain how they were found and assessed -- give the search strategies and selection criteria, describe how risk of bias was judged, and say whether the reviews were updated.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Referenced reviews with search strategy, selection criteria, risk-of-bias assessment, and update status

### Item 12 -- Assessment of the certainty of the body of evidence

**Description:** Describe the method used to rate the certainty of the body of evidence.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Certainty rating approach named (for example, GRADE) and applied to the evidence

## Recommendations (items 13-15)

### Item 13a -- Recommendations

**Description:** Give recommendations that are clear, precise, and actionable.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Recommendation statements specific enough to act on

### Item 13b -- Recommendations

**Description:** Give separate recommendations for important subgroups when the evidence indicates that the factors shaping recommendations differ, especially the balance of benefits and harms across subgroups.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Subgroup-specific recommendations where the benefit-harm balance differs

### Item 13c -- Recommendations

**Description:** State each recommendation's strength and the certainty of the evidence supporting it.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Strength-of-recommendation and certainty labels attached to each recommendation

### Item 14a -- Rationale/explanation for recommendations

**Description:** Say whether the target population's values and preferences fed into each recommendation; if they did, describe how they were elicited or identified; if not, explain why.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Values-and-preferences input documented per recommendation

### Item 14b -- Rationale/explanation for recommendations

**Description:** Say whether costs and resource implications entered into the recommendations; if so, describe the approaches used (such as cost-effectiveness analysis) and summarize the results; if not, explain why.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Cost and resource considerations (or their absence) explained

### Item 14c -- Rationale/explanation for recommendations

**Description:** Note any further factors weighed when the recommendations were formulated, such as equity, feasibility, and acceptability.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Additional considerations (equity, feasibility, acceptability) described

### Item 15 -- Evidence to decision processes

**Description:** Describe how the development group made its decisions, especially how recommendations were formulated -- for example how consensus was defined and reached and whether voting was used.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Decision-making process (consensus method, voting) documented

## Review and Quality Assurance (items 16-17)

### Item 16 -- External review

**Description:** State whether an independent review examined the draft guideline and, if so, how it was run and how the comments were weighed and addressed.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- External review process and comment disposition recorded

### Item 17 -- Quality assurance

**Description:** State whether a quality assurance process was applied to the guideline and, if so, describe it.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- QA process (for example, an appraisal of the draft guideline) described

## Funding and Declaration and Management of Interests (items 18-19)

### Item 18a -- Funding source(s) and role(s) of the funder

**Description:** Give the specific funding sources for each stage of guideline development.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Funding statement covering all development stages

### Item 18b -- Funding source(s) and role(s) of the funder

**Description:** Describe what role the funder(s) played in each stage of development and in disseminating and implementing the recommendations.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Funder involvement in development and dissemination stated

### Item 19a -- Declaration and management of interests

**Description:** State which kinds of conflicts, financial and nonfinancial, were relevant to the guideline's development.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Conflict-of-interest scope defined for the development group

### Item 19b -- Declaration and management of interests

**Description:** Explain how conflicts of interest were assessed and managed, and where users can find the declarations.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Interest-management process and public declaration access described

## Other Information (items 20-22)

### Item 20 -- Access

**Description:** State where the guideline, its appendices, and related documents can be obtained.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Access points (URLs, repositories) for the guideline and supplements

### Item 21 -- Suggestions for further research

**Description:** Describe the evidence gaps and/or suggest directions for future research.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Research-gap section with future-research suggestions

### Item 22 -- Limitations of the guideline

**Description:** Describe any limitations of the development process (for example a non-multidisciplinary development group or patient values and preferences that were not sought) and how they might have affected the validity of the recommendations.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Development-limitations section linked to the recommendations' validity
