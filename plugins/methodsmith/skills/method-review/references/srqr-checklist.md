# SRQR Checklist for Qualitative Research

Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research (SRQR).
21-item checklist (items S1-S21) for reporting qualitative studies of any approach -- ethnography, grounded theory, case study, phenomenology, or narrative research -- not only interviews and focus groups.

**Primary reference:** O'Brien BC, Harris IB, Beckman TJ, Reed DA, Cook DA. Standards for reporting qualitative research: a synthesis of recommendations. Acad Med. 2014;89(9):1245-1251. doi:10.1097/ACM.0000000000000388. PMID: 24979285.

**Source and licence:** Item numbers (S1-S21), section grouping, and item topics were verified on 2026-10-19 against Table 1 of O'Brien et al. 2014. The Acad Med article is copyright the Association of American Medical Colleges and carries no open licence (checked 2026-10-19 via Crossref; no Creative Commons licence is recorded for it), so the item descriptions below are faithful paraphrases that keep every element of the official item. Use the journal paper or the EQUATOR-hosted SRQR materials for verbatim wording. SRQR and COREQ overlap for interview and focus-group studies: apply one of the two, chosen with a stated reason (journal requirement or design fit), never both stacked. The "What to look for" bullets are methodsmith review guidance, not SRQR content.

Each item includes the item number, topic, description, and what to look for in analysis code and outputs when evaluating compliance.

## Title and Abstract (items S1-S2)

### Item S1 -- Title

**Description:** A concise description of the nature and topic of the study; identifying it as qualitative, naming the approach (for example ethnography or grounded theory), or naming the data collection methods (for example interview or focus group) is recommended.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Title or report header naming the study as qualitative with its approach or methods

### Item S2 -- Abstract

**Description:** A summary of the study's key elements following the intended publication's abstract format, typically covering background, purpose, methods, results, and conclusions.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Structured abstract covering all key elements

## Introduction (items S3-S4)

### Item S3 -- Problem formulation

**Description:** A statement of the problem or phenomenon studied and its significance, a review of the relevant theory and empirical work, and the problem statement.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Introduction stating the problem, its significance, and the theoretical grounding

### Item S4 -- Purpose or research question

**Description:** The study's purpose and its specific objectives or questions.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Explicit purpose statement and research questions

## Methods (items S5-S15)

### Item S5 -- Qualitative approach and research paradigm

**Description:** The qualitative approach (for example ethnography, grounded theory, case study, phenomenology, narrative research) and the guiding theory where appropriate; naming the research paradigm (for example postpositivist or constructivist/interpretivist) is also recommended, with rationale.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Approach named with its guiding theory and, where stated, the research paradigm
- Rationale for the chosen approach

### Item S6 -- Researcher characteristics and reflexivity

**Description:** The researchers' characteristics that could influence the research -- personal attributes, qualifications and experience, relationship with participants, assumptions or presuppositions -- and any potential or actual interaction between those characteristics and the research questions, approach, methods, results, or transferability.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Reflexivity statement covering researcher characteristics and their possible influence

### Item S7 -- Context

**Description:** The setting or site and its salient contextual factors, with rationale.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Study setting and context described

### Item S8 -- Sampling strategy

**Description:** How and why participants, documents, or events were selected, and the criteria for deciding that no further sampling was needed (for example sampling saturation), with rationale.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Sampling method and stopping criteria (for example, saturation) described

### Item S9 -- Ethical issues pertaining to human subjects

**Description:** Evidence of approval from an appropriate ethics review board and of participant consent, or an explanation of its absence, plus other confidentiality and data security issues.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Ethics approval reference and consent process documented

### Item S10 -- Data collection methods

**Description:** The types of data collected and the details of collection procedures, including (as appropriate) start and stop dates of collection and analysis, the iterative process, triangulation of sources or methods, and modification of procedures as findings evolved, with rationale.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Collection methods, dates, iteration, triangulation, and changes described

### Item S11 -- Data collection instruments and technologies

**Description:** A description of the instruments (for example interview guides, questionnaires) and devices (for example audio recorders) used, and whether and how they changed during the study.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Instruments named, with any mid-study changes

### Item S12 -- Units of study

**Description:** How many participants, documents, or events the study included and their relevant characteristics, plus the level of participation (may be reported in results instead).

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Counts and characteristics of participants, documents, or events

### Item S13 -- Data processing

**Description:** How the data were processed before and during analysis, including transcription, data entry, data management and security, verification of data integrity, coding, and anonymization or deidentification of excerpts.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Transcription, management, security, coding, and deidentification steps described

### Item S14 -- Data analysis

**Description:** How inferences, themes, and the like were identified and developed, naming the researchers involved in the analysis; usually tied to a specific paradigm or approach, with rationale.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Analysis process and analysts named, tied to the stated approach
- Analysis code (coding scripts, memos) consistent with the described process

### Item S15 -- Techniques to enhance trustworthiness

**Description:** The techniques used to strengthen the trustworthiness and credibility of the analysis (for example member checking, audit trail, triangulation), with rationale.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Trustworthiness techniques named and applied

## Results/Findings (items S16-S17)

### Item S16 -- Synthesis and interpretation

**Description:** The main findings -- interpretations, inferences, and themes -- which may include the development of a theory or model or integration with earlier research or theory.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Findings presented with themes and interpretations
- Theme structure consistent with the coded data

### Item S17 -- Links to empirical data

**Description:** Evidence (for example quotes, field notes, text excerpts, photographs) that backs the analytic findings.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Illustrative excerpts linked to themes, with attribution to sources

## Discussion (items S18-S19)

### Item S18 -- Integration with prior work, implications, transferability, and contribution(s) to the field

**Description:** A short recap of the main findings; how the findings and conclusions relate to earlier scholarship (connecting with, supporting, elaborating on, or challenging it); the scope of application or generalizability; and the study's unique contribution(s) to its discipline or field.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Discussion situating findings in prior work and stating contributions and applicability

### Item S19 -- Limitations

**Description:** The trustworthiness of the findings and their limitations.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Limitations section addressing trustworthiness of findings

## Other (items S20-S21)

### Item S20 -- Conflicts of interest

**Description:** Potential sources of influence, or perceived influence, on the study's conduct and conclusions, and how they were managed.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Conflicts of interest disclosed and management described

### Item S21 -- Funding

**Description:** The sources of funding and other support, and the funders' role in data collection, interpretation, and reporting.

**What to look for in code/outputs:**
- Funding sources and funder roles stated
